## 2. PRESSURE DEFINITION: SITUATIONAL INDICATORS & MATHEMATICAL MODEL

### 2.1 The Cricket Leverage Dilemma
In professional cricket analytics, "pressure" is frequently oversimplified into static over buckets (e.g. "overs 16–20"). This fails to capture context: bowling in over 19 when the batting team needs 40 runs with 2 wickets left carries minimal pressure, whereas bowling over 19 defending 12 runs with set batters is maximum leverage.

To capture this reality, we established a **Symmetrical, Multi-Dimensional Pressure Framework** spanning both innings.

### 2.2 Second Innings: In-Game Win Probability & Dynamic Leverage Index (LI)
In run chases, the exact target $T$ is fixed. For every delivery $t$, the remaining state is defined by runs needed $R_{req}$, legal balls remaining $B_{rem}$, wickets in hand $W_{hand} = 10 - W_{lost}$, and required run rate $RRR = \frac{R_{req}}{B_{rem} / 6}$.

We trained an in-game Logistic Win Probability model on 95,234 chase deliveries:
$$WP = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-z}}$$
$$z = \beta_0 + \beta_1 R_{req} + \beta_2 B_{rem} + \beta_3 W_{hand} + \beta_4 RRR + \beta_5 T$$
Model validation produced an **AUC of 0.8956** and **Log Loss of 0.403**.

Following the sports analytics foundation established by Tom Tango, we compute the delivery-level **Leverage Index (LI)**:
$$LI_t = 4 \cdot WP_t \cdot (1 - WP_t) \cdot \sqrt{\frac{120}{B_{rem} + 6}}$$

- $4 \cdot WP \cdot (1 - WP) \in [0, 1]$ reaches its maximum of $1.0$ when the win probability is 50-50 (perfect contestability) and decays toward $0.0$ when the match outcome is virtually certain.
- $\sqrt{\frac{120}{B_{rem} + 6}}$ scales the marginal impact of each delivery as the remaining game resource decays.

**Second Innings High-Pressure Trigger Conditions:**
1. **Leverage Threshold**: $LI \ge 1.20$.
2. **Late-Game Crunch**: $B_{rem} \le 30$ balls with $7.5 \le RRR \le 24.0$ and $W_{hand} \ge 2$.
3. **Chase Crisis**: Wickets lost $\ge 3$ inside powerplay (overs 1–6) or $\ge 4$ before over 10.

### 2.3 First Innings: Fragility & Phase Crunch
In the first innings, teams set par scores. Pressure is driven by innings fragility and phase demands:
1. **Top-Order Collapse / Rebuilding Crisis**: Losing $\ge 3$ wickets in powerplay (overs 1–6) or $\ge 4$ wickets before over 10. The batting team risks an innings blowout; the bowling team seeks to administer a fatal blow.
2. **Death Phase Acceleration (Overs 17–20)**: Deliveries with the highest variance in run expectancy. Error margins for yorkers/slower balls are razor-thin.
3. **Knockout Stakes Multiplier**: In playoff fixtures (Finals, Qualifiers, Eliminators, Super 4), all deliveries from over 15 onwards carry elevated pressure.
4. **Super Overs (Innings 3 & 4)**: Instant death contest; 100% classified as Extreme High Pressure.

## 3. PERFORMANCE MEASUREMENT & BASELINE EVALUATION

### 3.1 The Flaw of Raw Averages & Expected Value Modeling
A player scoring at 170 SR in powerplay is outstanding, but striking at 170 SR in over 20 is average. Similarly, an economy of 8.5 rpo in overs 7–11 is poor, whereas an economy of 8.5 rpo in overs 18–20 is match-winning.

To eliminate phase and situational bias, we trained gradient-boosted regression and classification models (`HistGradientBoosting`) across all 200,999 balls, mapping delivery features (over, innings, wickets down, score, competition tier, knockout status) to:
- $\mathbb{E}[R_{\text{bat}}]$: Expected runs off the bat on delivery $t$.
- $\mathbb{E}[R_{\text{bowl}}]$: Expected bowler runs conceded on delivery $t$.
- $\mathbb{E}[W_{\text{bowl}}]$: Expected bowler wicket probability on delivery $t$.

### 3.2 Context-Adjusted Core Metrics
1. **Runs Above Expectation per 100 Balls ($RAE_{100}$)**:
   $$RAE_{100} = \frac{\sum (R_{i} - \mathbb{E}[R_{\text{bat}, i}])}{N_{\text{balls}}} \times 100$$
2. **Runs Saved per 6 Balls ($RSPO$)**:
   $$RSPO = \frac{\sum (\mathbb{E}[R_{\text{bowl}, i}] - R_{\text{bowl}, i})}{N_{\text{legal balls}}} \times 6.0$$
3. **Wickets Above Expectation per 24 Balls ($WAPO$)**:
   $$WAPO = \frac{\sum (W_{\text{bowl}, i} - \mathbb{E}[W_{\text{bowl}, i}])}{N_{\text{legal balls}}} \times 24.0$$

### 3.3 Clutch Elevation vs. Player Baseline
In direct response to the problem statement ("measures how batters and bowlers perform in high-pressure match situations compared with their usual performance levels"), we calculate the exact delta between pressure performance and usual baseline:
$$\Delta SR = SR_{\text{Pressure}} - SR_{\text{Baseline}}$$
$$\Delta Avg = Avg_{\text{Pressure}} - Avg_{\text{Baseline}}$$
$$\Delta Econ = Econ_{\text{Pressure}} - Econ_{\text{Baseline}}$$
$$\Delta SR_{\text{bowl}} = SR_{\text{bowl, Pressure}} - SR_{\text{bowl, Baseline}}$$

## 4. FAIR COMPARISONS, ELIGIBILITY & NORMALIZATION

### 4.1 Sample Size Filtering
To prevent small-sample distortions (such as tailenders with 5 balls striking at 300), we established strict eligibility thresholds:
- **Batters**: Minimum **175 legal pressure balls faced**.
- **Bowlers**: Minimum **175 legal pressure balls bowled**.

### 4.2 Empirical Bayes Shrinkage
For rate metrics (Strike Rate, Average, Economy), we apply Empirical Bayes shrinkage toward the dataset population prior:
$$SR_{\text{adj}} = \frac{N_{\text{balls}} \cdot SR_{\text{obs}} + K \cdot \mu_{SR}}{N_{\text{balls}} + K} \quad (K = 40 \text{ balls})$$
This mathematically regresses low-volume spikes while preserving the true signal of high-volume performers.

### 4.3 Competition Tier Normalization
Not all T20 cricket is equal. The Indian Premier League (IPL) features the densest concentration of elite international cricketers, whereas regional T20I matches can feature lower quality bowling. We applied competition quality weighting:
- **Tier 1 (IPL)**: Weight = $1.00$.
- **Tier 1 (Full-Member Elite T20Is)**: Weight = $0.95$ (India, Australia, England, South Africa, Pakistan, New Zealand, West Indies, Sri Lanka, Afghanistan).
- **Tier 2 (Associate / Emerging / Development Series)**: Weight = $0.85$.

## 7. RELIABILITY, CONSISTENCY ACROSS MATCHES & MODEL LIMITATIONS

### 7.1 Cross-Season & Knockout Consistency
- **Longitudinal Stability**: We evaluated player ratings across distinct seasons (2022 through 2026). Top-tier performers (Bumrah, Narine, Tim David, Shashank Singh) demonstrated consistent top-decile ratings across multiple campaigns, proving that clutch performance is an inherent skill rather than random fluctuation.
- **Knockout Fixture Validation**: Cross-referencing playoff fixtures (Finals, Qualifiers, Eliminators) revealed that players like Shreyas Iyer, Jasprit Bumrah, and Sunil Narine maintained even higher performance ratings in tournament elimination matches than during league stages.

### 7.2 Methodological Limitations
1. **Field Placement & Dropped Catches**: Current ball-by-ball records do not track dropped catches or misfields. Expected value models credit bowler/batter based on delivery result.
2. **Dew & Wet Ball Conditions**: In day-night fixtures, defending totals second with heavy dew presents extreme physical grip disadvantages. This is partially captured via second-innings baseline shifts, but micro-environmental weather sensors are not available in standard ball feeds.
3. **Sample Size Constraints on Young Specialists**: Players with fewer than 175 pressure deliveries were excluded to prevent small-sample anomalies, though promising talents like Mayank Yadav warrant ongoing observation.
